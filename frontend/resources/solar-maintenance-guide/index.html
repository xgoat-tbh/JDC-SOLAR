Solar Panel Maintenance & Cleaning: Preventive Care for Maximum Generation

Environmental dust and industrial particulates in Eastern India can cause up to 15–20% soiling losses. Follow JDC Solar's engineering checklist to maintain peak plant yield and safeguard module warranties.

1. Recommended Cleaning Frequency & Timing

In industrial and suburban belts across Jamshedpur, Ranchi, and Dhanbad, airborne dust and soot settle on module anti-reflective glass.

  • Residential Plants: Clean panels every 15 to 20 days.
  • Industrial / Manufacturing Zones: Clean panels every 7 to 10 days due to higher particulate matter.
  • Timing Rule: Always clean in early morning (before 8:30 AM) or late evening (after 5:30 PM). Cleaning hot panels with cold water causes thermal shock and can micro-crack the photovoltaic glass.

2. Water Quality & Cleaning Tools

Never use hard ground water with high Total Dissolved Solids (TDS > 200 ppm), as hard water leaves mineral scale on the tempered glass that permanently reduces optical transmittance.

  • Use low-pressure demineralized (DM) or RO water.
  • Use soft microfiber mops or non-abrasive rubber squeegees.
  • Never use harsh chemical detergents, acid cleaners, or wire brushes.

3. Inverter & Electrical Preventive Health Checks

Regular electrical inspections protect against power loss and fire hazards:

  • Inverter Air Vents: Clean cooling heat-sinks and fan intake grilles every quarter to avoid thermal de-rating.
  • DC Isolators & Connectors: Inspect MC4 connectors for weather seals and thermal degradation.
  • Earthing Pit Resistance: Measure chemical earth pit resistance annually; value must remain strictly below 5.0 Ohms.

4. Thermal Hotspot Inspection

Bird droppings, fallen leaves, or localized shade cause individual shaded solar cells to act as electrical resistors rather than generators. This creates localized thermal hotspots exceeding 100°C that can permanently damage the backsheet. JDC Solar performs annual infrared thermography scans for all AMC clients.
