How Rooftop Solar Works: Net-Metering, Inverters & Grid Synchronization

A clear engineering explanation of how photovoltaic silicon modules generate DC power, how on-grid inverters synchronize with state electricity grids, and how bidirectional net-meters reduce electric bills by up to 90%.

1. The Photovoltaic (PV) Effect

Solar panels are constructed from semi-conducting silicon photovoltaic (PV) cells. When photons from sunlight strike the silicon cells, they knock electrons free from their atomic structure. High-conductivity busbars collect these free electrons, generating a flow of Direct Current (DC) electricity.

Modern installations engineered by JDC Solar utilize Mono PERC (Passivated Emitter and Rear Cell) and Bifacial TOPCon technology with efficiency ratings reaching 21.5% to 22.8%, generating maximum electricity even under hazy or overcast atmospheric conditions in Eastern India.

2. The Solar Inverter: DC to AC Conversion

Standard domestic and industrial appliances operate on Alternating Current (AC) at 230V single-phase or 415V three-phase with a precise 50 Hz frequency. The solar grid-tied inverter performs two critical functions:

  • Power Conversion: Inverts direct current (DC) from the roof into pure sine-wave AC power.
  • Grid Synchronization: Matches the voltage, phase angle, and 50 Hz frequency of the local utility grid (e.g. JBVNL or TSUISL).

3. Bidirectional Net-Metering Explained

In a grid-connected solar setup, your building remains continuously connected to the utility distribution lines. When solar panels generate more power than your property is consuming (typically between 10:00 AM and 3:00 PM), the surplus electricity flows backward through a Bidirectional Net-Meter into the public grid.

The Net-Billing Equation:
Billed Units = Energy Imported (from Grid) - Energy Exported (from Solar)
If your solar generation exceeds your consumption across a month, surplus energy credits rollover into your subsequent month's electricity bill.

4. Anti-Islanding Safety Protection

All grid-tied inverters feature certified Anti-Islanding Protection (conforming to IEEE 1547 / IEC 62116). During a utility power blackout, the solar inverter automatically disconnects within milliseconds to ensure zero electricity is exported into dead transmission lines, protecting linemen performing emergency repairs.
